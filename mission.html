The Founding Principle

The Sincere Strategy®
is structural, not stylistic.

Every principle below is also a business rule. Competitors built on ad revenue, affiliate fees, or data sales cannot adopt these rules without dismantling their own revenue model. The moat compounds with every household we serve.

🚫
Zero paid placements. Ever. Revenue is subscriptions only. We never take money to influence what you buy. No brand can pay to appear in a WhollyFare plan, a WhollyWare recommendation, or any Wholly product.
🛡️
Health before savings, always. Dietary constraints are absolute hard rules, never traded for a better deal. The constraint engine runs before the optimizer. A $0.50 saving never overrides a household member's allergen profile or health condition.
👁️
Radical transparency. Every ingredient rejection is logged and shown to you. If something was excluded from your plan, you see exactly why. No black boxes. Radical transparency is not a feature — it's a founding principle.
📍
Local-first, always. Plans built from your actual stores' actual sale circulars, not national averages, not generic estimates. Your town, your stores, your prices. The week's plan is built from what's actually on sale where you actually shop.
⛽
Honest trip costs. We show you net savings after gas. We flag stores where savings don't cover the drive — even if it means a lower savings headline. Don't burn gas to save pennies. We say so when it happens.
🔒
Your data is yours. We know your household's health constraints, dietary needs, and spending habits. That data stays yours. Never sold, never shared, never used for targeting. Ever.

Why It's Durable

Competitors cannot copy this
without destroying their own model.

The Sincere Strategy is not a positioning choice. It's a structural commitment that makes certain revenue streams permanently off-limits. That constraint is exactly what makes it a moat — and exactly why incumbents can't follow.

The same logic applies to consulting. When an advisor's recommendations are shaped by vendor relationships, software commissions, or a bias toward billable complexity, the client pays twice — once in fees, once in bad outcomes. Sentir Consulting is built without those dependencies. The moat is the same: sincerity is structural, and incumbents cannot adopt it without dismantling their own model.

"Affiliates, sponsored placements, and data sales are our competitors' business model. Ours is you paying us a fair subscription because we save you more than we cost. That's the asymmetry. It's structural and it's durable."

— Sentir Solutions founding thesis, May 2026

The incumbent problem

Most grocery and household savings tools — coupon apps, store loyalty programs, cashback services — are funded by the retailers and brands they claim to help you compare. Their incentive is to drive you toward higher-margin products and sponsored placements, not to find you the best deal.

Why they can't switch

To adopt the Sincere Strategy, a competitor would have to eliminate affiliate revenue, refuse all paid placements, and return user data to the household. That's not a product update — it's a business model replacement. Their investors won't allow it. Their growth metrics depend on it. The moat is real.

Why subscriptions work

A household that saves $1,500 per year from WhollyFare's Full Table tier pays $348 per year for the subscription. The value case is undeniable. When savings exceed cost by 4x, retention is structural — not dependent on lock-in, habit loops, or dark patterns.
